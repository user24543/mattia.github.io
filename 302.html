( 01 · the map )

the first animal whose whole nervous system was mapped, cell by cell.

302neurons
—pairs joined by chemical synapses
—pairs joined by gap junctions
—neurons that reach muscle

in 1986 john white, sydney brenner and colleagues published the wiring of caenorhabditis elegans, traced by hand through thousands of electron micrographs over more than a decade. it was the first complete connectome of any animal.

the map on this page is a newer reading of the same animal by cook and colleagues in 2019, which redid the hermaphrodite and added the male, including every connection to muscle.

there are two kinds of line. a chemical synapse sends a signal one way, from one cell to the next. a gap junction is a direct electrical pore between two cells, and current can flow either way through it.

the activity you see is a simple model running on those real connections. the wiring is real; how strong each connection is, and whether it excites or calms, is mostly guessed.

( 03 · what the map leaves out )

03 / 03
i.

no plus or minus

the map says which cells touch, and how many synapses join them. it does not say whether each synapse excites or calms the next cell, and the same chemical can do either depending on the receptor.

ii.

a second, wireless map

neuropeptides and amines such as serotonin drift between cells that never touch. mapped in 2023, that chemical network is denser than the wiring itself.

iii.

one map, many moods

a hungry worm and a fed worm have the same wiring and behave differently. state changes which connections matter, minute to minute.

iv.

still not solved

nearly forty years after the first map, no simulation fully reproduces what this worm does. having every wire is a start, not an answer.
