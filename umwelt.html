( 01 · the idea )

01 / 03

every animal lives inside a bubble made of what it can sense.

in 1934 the biologist jakob von uexküll gave that bubble a name: the umwelt, german for "the world around". a tick, a bee and a dog standing in the same garden do not share a world. each picks up a handful of signals and ignores the rest, and that handful is everything that exists for it.

the garden at the top of this page never changes. the mouse runs the same loop, the moth circles, the stone gives back the day's heat. only the reader changes. switching senses never shows the real garden underneath; it swaps one partial picture for another, and that includes yours.

( 03 · lost in translation )

03 / 03
i.

false colour

a bee has no blue for ultraviolet. drawing it as blue translates it into a colour you already have. the bee's own colour for it is one no human has seen.

ii.

hearing drawn as light

the bat's garden is made of sound. glowing rings keep the timing of each echo but lose the texture a bat hears in every return.

iii.

one sense at a time

real animals blend their senses. a snake's brain lays heat over sight in a single map, and a dog sees and smells at once. this page pulls them apart so each one can be noticed.

iv.

what is it like to be a bat?

in 1974 the philosopher thomas nagel argued that you could learn every fact about echolocation and still not know what it is like from the inside. this page is a set of facts. the inside stays out of reach.
